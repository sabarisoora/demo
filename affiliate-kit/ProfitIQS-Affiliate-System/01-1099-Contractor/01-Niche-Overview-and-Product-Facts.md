## Audience

Independent contractors, freelancers and self-employed professionals in the US who are paid on 1099s.

## Buyer profiles (HYPOTHESIS)

**The first-year freelancer.** Recently went independent, still figuring out quarterly taxes, and keeps income in one bank account and receipts in a phone.

**The established solo consultant.** Several regular clients and steady income, but no clear view of which clients or services are worth the time.

**The mileage-heavy contractor.** Drives to clients or job sites and needs mileage and expenses kept tidy for tax time.

**The side-business earner.** Has a 1099 income stream next to a job and wants the side business to be organized from day one.

## Business problems in this industry (HYPOTHESIS)

General industry context to shape your angle. Do not present these as facts about the buyer or results of the product.

- **Irregular income, unclear profit.** Payments arrive on different schedules from different clients, so it is hard to know what a month actually earned after expenses.
- **Not knowing which clients pay off.** A client who sends the most work is not always the one who earns the most once time and expenses are counted.
- **Quarterly estimated taxes.** Without employer withholding, contractors have to plan for estimated taxes themselves, and the bill can be a surprise.
- **Mileage and expenses left untracked.** Deductible expenses and mileage are easy to lose when they are not logged as they happen.
- **Unpaid invoices.** Invoices that slip past their due date quietly turn into a cash-flow problem.
- **Year-end scramble.** Gathering a year of income, expenses and mileage for the tax preparer takes days when nothing is organized.

## Buyer motivations (HYPOTHESIS)

- Know what they actually earn after expenses and taxes
- See which clients and services are worth the time
- Set aside the right amount for estimated taxes
- Keep mileage and deductions organized all year
- Hand the tax preparer a clean year-end package

Whether it helps depends on the owner entering data and reviewing it regularly.

## Key numbers this audience cares about (educational)

- **Net profit after expenses:** What is left from client payments once business expenses are taken out.
- **Client profitability:** How much each client earns you once the time and costs of their work are counted.
- **Estimated tax set-aside:** The share of profit to put aside for quarterly estimated taxes so the bill is not a surprise.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money clients owe you for invoices already sent.
- **Business mileage:** Miles driven for business, logged as you go for your tax preparer.
- **Cash flow:** Money coming in and going out month by month, which can differ from profit when clients pay late.
