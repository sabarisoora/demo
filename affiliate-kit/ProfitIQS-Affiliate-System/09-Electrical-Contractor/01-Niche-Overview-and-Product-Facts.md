## Audience

Electrical contractors and electrical service business owners, from solo electricians to shops with several crews.

## Buyer profiles (HYPOTHESIS)

**The owner-electrician.** Still on the tools most days, quotes jobs from experience and invoices at night.

**The growing service shop.** Several electricians, a mix of service calls and projects, and no side-by-side view of who produces what.

**The project-focused contractor.** Bids larger jobs and needs to know which ones actually pay.

**The office manager.** Handles invoicing and receivables and wants a clear monthly picture.

## Business problems in this industry (HYPOTHESIS)

General industry context to shape your angle. Do not present these as facts about the buyer or results of the product.

- **Jobs that look good until materials land.** Material and labor costs arriving after the quote can erase a job's margin.
- **Electrician output is a guess.** Owners know who feels fastest, not whose hours produce the most revenue.
- **Leads that never turn into jobs.** Without tracking leads, it is hard to see which sources and follow-ups work.
- **Overdue invoices.** Receivables aging past 30, 60 or 90 days strain cash when payroll and suppliers are due.
- **No view of which customers pay off.** Some customers bring steady, profitable work; others do not.
- **Tax-time paperwork.** Materials, vans, tools and payroll costs end up scattered by year end.

## Buyer motivations (HYPOTHESIS)

- See which jobs and customers are profitable
- Compare electricians on real output
- Track leads through to jobs
- Get paid faster
- Be organized for the accountant

## Why someone might consider a business intelligence system

The common thread is visibility: electrical contractors usually have the data (repair orders, invoices, receipts) but not one place that turns it into decisions. The Electrical Contractor Business Intelligence System™ gives them a ready-built Excel workbook for that (VERIFIED: dashboards, reports and tax set-aside built in). Whether it helps depends on the owner entering data and reviewing it regularly.

## Key numbers this audience cares about (educational)

- **Job margin:** What a job keeps after materials and labor.
- **Revenue per electrician:** How much billed work each electrician produces.
- **Lead conversion:** The share of leads that become paid jobs.
- **AR aging:** Unpaid invoices grouped by how long they have been outstanding.
- **Average job value:** Typical revenue per job.
- **Cash flow:** Money in and out each month, which can differ from profit when customers pay late.
