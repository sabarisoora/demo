## Audience

General contractors, remodelers and construction business owners who manage project budgets.

## Buyer profiles (HYPOTHESIS)

**The remodeling contractor.** Runs several residential projects at once and estimates from experience.

**The small general contractor.** Manages subcontractors, change orders and progress billing across a handful of jobs.

**The owner-operator builder.** Works on site during the day and does job costing at night, if at all.

**The office manager.** Keeps the books and wants a clean view of project profit and work in progress.

## Business problems in this industry (HYPOTHESIS)

General industry context to shape your angle. Do not present these as facts about the buyer or results of the product.

- **Jobs that look profitable until they close.** Costs land after the estimate and the real margin shows up only when the project is finished.
- **Change orders that never get billed.** Extra work agreed on site can go unpriced or unbilled.
- **Work in progress blind spots.** Billing ahead of or behind the work done distorts how healthy the business looks.
- **Cash flow between draws.** Paying crews and suppliers before the next payment arrives strains cash.
- **No comparison across projects.** Without a portfolio view, it is hard to see which kinds of job actually pay.
- **Tax-time paperwork.** Job costs, receipts and subcontractor payments end up scattered by year end.

## Why someone might consider a business intelligence system

The common thread is visibility: contractors usually have the data (repair orders, invoices, receipts) but not one place that turns it into decisions. The Construction Job Cost Business Intelligence System™ gives them a ready-built Excel workbook for that (VERIFIED: dashboards, reports and tax set-aside built in). Whether it helps depends on the owner entering data and reviewing it regularly.

## Key numbers this audience cares about (educational)

- **Job cost to date:** Everything spent on a project so far, against its budget.
- **Gross margin per project:** What a project keeps after its direct costs, as a share of the contract.
- **Work in progress (WIP):** Whether you have billed more or less than the work actually completed.
- **Change order value:** Extra work agreed after the original contract.
- **Backlog:** Contracted work not yet completed.
- **Accounts receivable:** Money owed on invoices and draws already billed.
